## Subscription only, since 2016

Autodesk stopped selling new perpetual licences for AutoCAD in 2016. Since then the only way to run it is a subscription — monthly, yearly or multi-year — which includes every update released while it's active. Cancel and the software stops opening drawings; your files stay yours (DWG is just a file), but the editor stops.

That change is why the alternatives market woke up, and why questions like [is there a portable AutoCAD](/blog/portable-autocad) mostly lead to cracked downloads — not a route to recommend.

## What the full subscription includes

The headline price buys more than the desktop program:

- **All the specialized toolsets.** One AutoCAD subscription includes Architecture, Electrical, MEP, Map 3D, Mechanical, Plant 3D, Raster Design — the toolsets that used to be separate products. For an office that needs two of them, this quietly halves the bill.
- **AutoCAD Web and the mobile app.** Full editing in a browser and on a phone or tablet — genuinely useful for site visits and quick markups, not just a viewer.
- **Updates**, continuously, plus cloud storage and the Autodesk account machinery.

## AutoCAD LT: the 2D edition

For pure 2D drafting, LT is the same program with the 3D modelling removed — and at roughly a quarter of the price, it's the value pick of the official lineup:

|                        | AutoCAD              | AutoCAD LT            |
| ---------------------- | -------------------- | --------------------- |
| 2D drafting, full DWG  | ✓                    | ✓                     |
| 3D solids and surfaces | ✓                    | —                     |
| Specialized toolsets   | ✓ all of them        | —                     |
| LISP and customization | ✓                    | ✓ back in recent LT   |
| Web and mobile apps    | ✓                    | ✓                     |
| Price                  | about 2,000 USD/year | about a quarter of it |

The catch is one-directional: if your work later needs 3D or a toolset, you upgrade to the full subscription; there's no discount for having had LT.

## The cheaper official doors

- **Education plan — free.** Students and teachers get the full AutoCAD free, renewable yearly, with an education watermark on plots. If you're learning, this plus [a study roadmap](/blog/how-to-learn-autocad) is the whole toolkit.
- **Flex — pay per day.** Autodesk's token system: buy tokens in packs, spend one per day you actually open the program. Below roughly a week of use per month, Flex costs less than a subscription. Above that, subscribe.
- **30-day trial.** Full program, free, once. Good for a project evaluation, not a lifestyle.

## Which one fits you

![Which option fits: an employer or client pays means full AutoCAD, the industry default with all toolsets; a student or teacher means the free education plan; 2D drafting on a budget means LT or the free CADCraft, LibreCAD and QCAD; only a few days of use a month means Flex tokens cost less than a subscription; if you mainly need to exchange DWG files, any DWG-capable editor works](/imgs/blog/autocad-pricing-picker.svg)

- **An employer or client is paying:** full AutoCAD. It's the industry default, the toolsets are included, and compatibility questions disappear.
- **You're a student or teacher:** the education plan. Free, renewable, the real program.
- **2D drafting on your own budget:** LT — or genuinely free software. CADCraft copies the AutoCAD command workflow and reads and writes DWG and DXF; [try it in the browser](/online) before installing anything.
- **A few days a month:** Flex tokens. Do the arithmetic against the subscription once a year.
- **You mostly need to exchange DWG files:** any editor that reads and writes DWG honestly — [which is a shorter list than marketing pages suggest](/blog/open-source-dwg-editor).

## The other cost: your time

Subscription money isn't the whole bill — retraining is. Moving to any alternative costs days of "where is that command", against months of subscription fees. It cuts both ways: AutoCAD skills transfer almost entirely to BricsCAD and CADCraft (same commands, aliases and prompts), and barely at all to a genuinely different paradigm. The cheapest long-term position is competence in the AutoCAD workflow itself — which free tools let you build without paying anyone. See [how to learn AutoCAD](/blog/how-to-learn-autocad).
