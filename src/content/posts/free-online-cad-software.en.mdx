**Short answer:** "online CAD" covers two different things. **In-browser CAD** downloads the program into the tab and runs it on your computer, so your file never leaves it. **Cloud CAD** keeps your model on the vendor's servers and streams the view to you. Pick by the job:

- **Quick 2D drawing or a DXF edit, without an account:** [CADCraft online](/online).
- **Parametric 3D parts, shared with a team:** Onshape (the free plan makes documents public).
- **Your first 3D models, 3D printing, a classroom:** Tinkercad.
- **Editing DWG with real AutoCAD tools:** AutoCAD Web, which needs a subscription after the trial.
- **Only looking at a DWG:** Autodesk Viewer or another free viewer.

_cadcraft.org is an independent guide, not the official site. CADCraft is the ArtCraft team's open-source CAD program ([getartcraft.com/apps/cadcraft](https://getartcraft.com/apps/cadcraft)). CADCraft details were checked against its 0.13.0 source; plans for the other tools are as of October 2026, so check their pricing pages before relying on them._

## In-browser vs cloud: the difference that matters

Both kinds open in a browser tab, so they look the same. Where the work happens decides almost everything else: privacy, accounts, offline use, and how big a drawing you can open.

![Two kinds of online CAD. In-browser WebAssembly CAD such as CADCraft online runs on your computer: the file stays local, often no account, works offline once loaded. Cloud CAD such as Onshape or AutoCAD Web runs on vendor servers: files are uploaded, an account is always needed, no offline use, sharing by link is built in](/imgs/blog/online-cad-where-it-runs.svg)

**In-browser (WebAssembly).** The CAD program is compiled to WebAssembly and downloaded once. Opening a file reads it from your disk into the tab, and saving downloads a new file. Nothing has to be uploaded, so no account is needed. The flip side: you share files the old way, by sending them, and a huge drawing is limited by your computer's memory.

**Cloud CAD.** The model lives on the vendor's servers. You sign in, upload or create files there, and collaborate by sharing a link. That's very convenient for teams, and a cheap laptop can open a big model. But it needs a connection, an account, and trust in the vendor's terms, especially on free plans.

Neither is better in general. For a client's drawing under NDA, in-browser is easier to defend. For three engineers working on one assembly, cloud is easier to live with.

Some notes the table can't hold:

- **Onshape** is a full parametric 3D CAD system and the strongest free option for mechanical parts. The catch is the free plan: documents are public and the licence is for non-commercial use, so it isn't for paid client work.
- **Tinkercad** is built for learning. You put boxes, cylinders and holes together, which is great for a first 3D print and quickly limiting for anything with tolerances.
- **AutoCAD Web** is real AutoCAD editing for DWG in a browser, with a reduced toolset. It's the right answer if the job is "edit this DWG exactly the way AutoCAD would" and someone pays for the subscription.
- **Viewers** (Autodesk Viewer, ShareCAD and others) are enough when you only need to look, measure and print. See [how to open a DWG file](/blog/how-to-open-dwg-file) for the free routes.

## CADCraft online: 2D CAD in a tab, no account

[CADCraft online](/online) is the same program as the CADCraft desktop app, compiled to WebAssembly. It has the AutoCAD-style command line, object snaps, layers, dimensions, hatches, blocks and paper-space layouts. Open the page and you're drawing in a few seconds; there's no sign-up.

![The CADCraft online editor in a browser with a 120 by 80 plate: a 30 mm centre hole with a diameter dimension, four corner holes on a red layer, overall width and height dimensions, the Tool Sets palette on the left and the command line at the bottom](/imgs/blog/online-cad-browser-plate.webp)

_Drawn for this article in CADCraft online by typing commands: `rectang 0,0 120,80`, `circle 60,40 d 30`, four corner circles, then `dimlinear` and `dimdiameter`._

You can try exactly that. Click in the drawing area and type each line, pressing Enter after it:

```text
rectang 0,0 120,80
circle 60,40 d 30
circle 10,10 d 6
dimlinear 0,0 120,0 60,-14
zoom e
```

If you know AutoCAD, the commands and aliases will feel familiar (`L` for LINE, `C` for CIRCLE, `@10<45` for relative polar input). The [AutoCAD commands cheat sheet](/blog/autocad-commands) works here too.

### What it does well

- **Privacy by design.** The drawing is processed in your tab. Nothing is uploaded to a server to open or save it.
- **No install.** Useful on a locked-down work or school computer, a Chromebook, or a borrowed laptop.
- **Real file output.** Save as DXF, which AutoCAD, LibreCAD, QCAD, FreeCAD and laser-cutter software all read. Export to SVG or PNG for documents and the web.
- **Same skills as the desktop app.** Anything you learn in the browser carries straight over to the [desktop download](/download).

### Where it falls short (read this before you start a real job)

- **DXF only.** The web build doesn't read or write DWG. Convert first ([DWG to DXF](/blog/dwg-to-dxf)), or use the desktop app, which opens DWG R13 to 2018.
- **No autosave.** The desktop app autosaves; the browser version doesn't yet. Save to DXF often. A closed tab loses unsaved work.
- **PDF plotting in the browser is unreliable.** The project lists a known issue where plotting shows raw PDF bytes. Export SVG or PNG, or plot from the desktop app.
- **Pre-alpha software.** CADCraft's own roadmap rates it about 38% ready for real work. It's fine for sketches, plates, simple plans and DXF fixes. Check anything critical before you send it.
- **2D only.** For 3D parts use Onshape, or a desktop tool like FreeCAD.

## Which one should you use?

**A drawing for a laser cutter, CNC router or plasma table.** These want DXF. Draw it in CADCraft online, check the units, save DXF. No account, no upload.

**A floor plan or garden layout.** CADCraft online for an accurate 2D plan with dimensions; [how to draw a floor plan](/blog/how-to-draw-a-floor-plan-in-autocad) walks through the method with AutoCAD-style commands.

**A part you'll 3D print.** Tinkercad if it's simple and you're new to CAD. Onshape if it needs real dimensions and later changes, and you don't mind it being public.

**A DWG from an architect that you must edit and send back as DWG.** That's the one case where free online tools struggle. AutoCAD Web, or the CADCraft desktop app if a lossy round trip is acceptable. [DXF vs DWG](/blog/dxf-vs-dwg) explains what gets lost.

**Just look at a drawing.** A viewer. Don't learn a CAD program to read a dimension.

## Tips for working in browser CAD

- **Save early, save often.** Download the DXF after every important step. Browser tabs crash, and laptops sleep.
- **Set units before you draw.** A drawing started in inches and sent to a metric shop is the most common mistake. In CADCraft, check `UNITS` before the first line.
- **Use a mouse with a wheel.** Panning and zooming with a trackpad works, but a wheel mouse is much faster for drafting.
- **Keep files on your computer, not in the browser.** Browser storage can be cleared at any time. Your Downloads folder can't, unless you clear it.
- **Learn ten commands.** LINE, CIRCLE, RECTANG, OFFSET, TRIM, MOVE, COPY, DIMLINEAR, LAYER and ZOOM cover most small jobs. The [AutoCAD shortcuts list](/blog/autocad-shortcuts) has the aliases.
