**Short answer:** there's no free AutoCAD, but there's a free tool for most jobs AutoCAD does. To **look at and print** a DWG, use Autodesk's own DWG TrueView (Windows). For **AutoCAD-style 2D drafting** on any computer, try CADCraft ([download](/download) or [online](/online)). For **simple 2D DXF work you get paid for**, LibreCAD or QCAD Community Edition. For **3D parts**, FreeCAD. If you're a **student or teacher**, get the real AutoCAD through Autodesk's education licence.

_cadcraft.org is an independent guide to CADCraft, not the official site and not affiliated with Autodesk. Facts about other products come from their own pages as of October 2026; licences and prices change, so check before you rely on them. CADCraft details were checked against its 0.13.0 source._

## "Free" means four different things

Before comparing features, sort the candidates by **what kind of free** they are. It decides whether you may use them for paid work at all.

![Four kinds of free CAD: open source (CADCraft, LibreCAD, QCAD Community, FreeCAD), free with terms (nanoCAD Free, Fusion personal use), free viewers (DWG TrueView, Autodesk Viewer) and free education licences (AutoCAD Education), each with its catch](/imgs/blog/free-autocad-alternatives-kinds.svg)

1. **Open source.** Free for any use, including commercial work, with no account. You give up vendor support and full DWG fidelity.
2. **Free with terms.** A commercial vendor gives away a limited edition. nanoCAD Free, for example, is "intended for non-commercial use", needs registration and a yearly (free) renewal, and supports DWG only up to 2013. Fusion's personal-use licence is non-commercial too.
3. **Free viewers.** DWG TrueView and Autodesk Viewer open drawings so you can view, measure and print them. You can't edit.
4. **Free for education.** The real AutoCAD, for eligible students and educators. Work you're paid for isn't allowed.

Two names that used to be on every "free AutoCAD" list no longer belong there. **DraftSight** has no free edition now, only a 30-day trial of DraftSight Premium. **Fusion** personal use is free but lost DWG and DXF export in Autodesk's 2020 licence change, and it is a 3D tool anyway.

## Which one, in five situations

**"Someone sent me a DWG and I need to check a dimension or print it."** On Windows, DWG TrueView does exactly this and comes from the company that defines the format. On a Mac or Linux, or to batch-convert a folder to PDF or PNG, use CADCraft: the desktop app opens the file, and `cadcraft-cli convert plan.dwg plan.pdf` does it without a window. [How to open a DWG file](/blog/how-to-open-dwg-file) walks through both.

**"I draft in AutoCAD every day and want the same habits for free."** CADCraft is built for this: you type `L`, `@5<45`, `TR`, use the same aliases, and work in paper-space layouts with viewports. Try it on real drawings before committing (see the test below), because it's young.

**"I make simple 2D DXF drawings for laser cutting or CNC, for clients."** LibreCAD and QCAD Community Edition are mature, light and licensed for commercial use. If your clients send DWG, LibreCAD reads it; QCAD needs the paid Professional edition.

**"I design parts that get machined or 3D printed."** FreeCAD. Model in 3D and make the 2D drawings from the model with its TechDraw workbench. Don't force 3D work into a 2D drafting tool.

**"I'm a student."** Use the education licence and learn on the real AutoCAD, because that's what job ads ask for. Keep a free tool for anything you sell.

## What you give up when you leave AutoCAD

This is the part most lists skip. Here's where CADCraft, the closest in workflow, stands today, from its own project documents:

| AutoCAD feature                 | CADCraft 0.13.0                                                                                                                                       |
| ------------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| DWG round trips                 | Opens R13–2018. A DWG it saves keeps only what CADCraft models, and the project says those files haven't been checked in AutoCAD. Keep your originals |
| SHX fonts                       | Not read. Text is drawn with CADCraft's own stroke font, or a TrueType font you set with `FONTALT`, so text widths differ                             |
| Plot styles (`.ctb`/`.stb`)     | Plot style tables in CADCraft's own format; AutoCAD's binary `.ctb` files aren't read. [DWG to PDF](/blog/dwg-to-pdf) shows how to rebuild one        |
| AutoLISP                        | `.lsp` routines load (APPLOAD); no `vlax-` ActiveX functions, no DCL dialogs, no `.fas`/`.vlx`, no ObjectARX plug-ins                                 |
| 3D                              | None yet. CADCraft draws in 2D plan view; `3DORBIT` answers "not available yet"                                                                       |
| Raster images and PDF underlays | Not shown; `ATTACH` isn't available yet                                                                                                               |
| Readiness                       | Pre-alpha. The roadmap estimates ≈ 38% "ready for real work" against AutoCAD 2027                                                                     |

What it adds that AutoCAD doesn't: a free licence with no account, Linux and FreeBSD builds, a [browser version](/online) that opens DXF, a command-line tool for batch jobs, and an MCP server so AI agents can draw (see [text to CAD](/blog/text-to-cad)).

The other tools have their own versions of this list. LibreCAD has no paper-space layouts. QCAD Community Edition has no DWG. FreeCAD's 2D drafting is a side of its 3D modeller. nanoCAD Free is a 2013-era release that its vendor doesn't promise will run on Windows 10 or 11.

## Test any alternative with your own files

An hour of testing beats any comparison table, this one included.

1. **Pick three real drawings:** a simple one, your largest, and one from an outside consultant.
2. **Open each one** and compare it with a PDF plotted from AutoCAD. Look at text, hatches, dimensions and blocks first. With CADCraft, `cadcraft-cli info drawing.dwg` lists entity counts and anything it skipped, before you even open the window.
3. **Plot a sheet to PDF** and check lineweights at 100% zoom.
4. **Save, reopen in AutoCAD** (or ask whoever sends you files to do it), and look for missing objects.
5. **Run your two most-used LISP routines or scripts**, if you have them.

If a drawing that matters fails step 4, keep AutoCAD (or DWG TrueView) for exchange and use the free tool for the work around it. Many people end up doing exactly that.
